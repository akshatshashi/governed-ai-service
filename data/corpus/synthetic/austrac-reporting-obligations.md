# AUSTRAC reporting obligations: summary for operations staff

Provenance: a plain-English summary written for this project, paraphrasing publicly available AUSTRAC guidance on the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2006 (AML/CTF Act). It is simplified and is not legal advice. The AML/CTF Amendment Act 2024 changed several obligations from 31 March 2026, so always check current AUSTRAC guidance before relying on this summary.

## Who has reporting obligations

A business that provides a designated service under the AML/CTF Act is a reporting entity. Designated services include many banking, remittance, foreign currency exchange, digital currency exchange, bullion and gambling services.

A reporting entity must enrol with AUSTRAC within 28 days of first providing a designated service. Remittance service providers must also be registered with AUSTRAC before they provide remittance services.

## Threshold transaction reports (TTRs)

A threshold transaction report (TTR) must be submitted when a reporting entity provides a designated service involving the transfer of physical currency (cash) of AUD 10,000 or more, or the foreign currency equivalent. The TTR must be submitted to AUSTRAC within 10 business days after the day of the transaction.

Physical currency means coins and paper money. Electronic transfers are not reported as TTRs. A TTR is required whether or not the transaction looks suspicious.

## Suspicious matter reports (SMRs)

A suspicious matter report (SMR) must be submitted when a reporting entity forms a suspicion on reasonable grounds that a customer is not who they claim to be, or that a transaction or activity may be related to money laundering, terrorism financing, tax evasion, the proceeds of crime or another serious offence.

A suspicious matter report (SMR) must be submitted to AUSTRAC within 3 business days after the reporting entity forms the suspicion. If the suspicion relates to the financing of terrorism, the SMR must be submitted within 24 hours.

There is no minimum dollar amount for an SMR. An SMR can be required even if the customer did not go ahead with the transaction.

## International funds transfer instructions (IFTIs)

An international funds transfer instruction (IFTI) report must be submitted to AUSTRAC within 10 business days after the instruction is sent or received. IFTIs cover instructions to transfer money or property into or out of Australia.

There is no minimum threshold for IFTI reporting: every international funds transfer instruction is reported, regardless of the amount.

## Cross-border movement of physical currency

A person carrying AUD 10,000 or more in physical currency (or the foreign currency equivalent) into or out of Australia must declare it to the Australian Border Force. Undeclared currency can be seized. Travellers must also disclose bearer negotiable instruments, such as traveller's cheques, if an officer asks.

## Annual compliance report

Each reporting entity must lodge an annual compliance report with AUSTRAC. The report covers the previous calendar year and is due by 31 March.

## Tipping off

A reporting entity must not disclose to anyone, other than AUSTRAC and certain permitted parties, that an SMR has been or will be submitted, or any information from which that could reasonably be inferred. Doing so is known as tipping off and is an offence.

## Structuring

Structuring means deliberately splitting cash transactions into amounts below AUD 10,000, or otherwise arranging transactions, so that a threshold transaction report is avoided. Structuring is an offence under the AML/CTF Act even when the money itself comes from a legitimate source. Staff must never suggest to a customer how to keep a transaction below the reporting threshold.
