# Customer due diligence (KYC): summary for operations staff

Provenance: a plain-English summary written for this project, paraphrasing publicly available AUSTRAC guidance on customer due diligence. It is simplified and is not legal advice; check current AUSTRAC guidance and the AML/CTF Rules before relying on it.

## Identify before you serve

A reporting entity must identify and verify a customer before providing a designated service, apart from limited exceptions set out in the AML/CTF Rules. The depth of checking should match the money laundering and terrorism financing risk the customer presents.

## Minimum checks for individual customers

For an individual customer, collect at least the customer's full name and either their date of birth or their residential address. Verify the collected details using reliable and independent documents, such as a passport or driver licence, or reliable and independent electronic data.

## Beneficial owners

For a company, trust or other non-individual customer, identify the beneficial owners. A beneficial owner is an individual who ultimately owns 25% or more of the customer, or who otherwise controls it. Take reasonable steps to verify each beneficial owner.

## Politically exposed persons (PEPs)

A politically exposed person holds a prominent public position, or is an immediate family member or close associate of such a person. PEPs can be domestic, foreign, or from an international organisation.

Foreign PEPs must always be treated as high risk. For a high-risk PEP, obtain senior management approval before starting or continuing the relationship, take reasonable steps to establish the source of wealth and source of funds, and apply enhanced ongoing monitoring.

## Enhanced customer due diligence

Enhanced customer due diligence is required when the money laundering or terrorism financing risk is high, when the customer is a foreign PEP, or when a party to the transaction is connected to a jurisdiction identified as high risk. Enhanced measures include collecting more information, verifying the source of funds, and increasing how often the relationship is reviewed.

## Ongoing customer due diligence

Customer due diligence does not end at onboarding. A reporting entity must monitor customers and their transactions on an ongoing basis, and update customer information when it changes or when the risk profile changes.

## Sanctions screening

Australian sanctions laws are administered by the Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade (DFAT). Customers and payment counterparties should be screened against the DFAT Consolidated List before a transaction is processed. Dealing with the assets of a designated person or entity is prohibited.
